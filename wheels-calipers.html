Overview

Wheels date a car faster than paint does.

Curb rash, brake dust staining and peeling factory clear on the wheels will make an otherwise immaculate vehicle look tired. It is also the cheapest area to transform.

Connecticut roads are hard on wheels. Potholes, curbs and winter salt all take their toll, and factory wheel finishes are thinner than most people assume. Once the clear coat lifts at a curb scrape, corrosion works underneath it and spreads.

Refinishing repairs the damage and puts a proper finish back on. Powder coating goes further, applying a dry finish that is cured onto the wheel and is considerably more durable than wet paint against chips, brake heat and salt.

Color is a real decision, not just a repair. Gloss black, satin black, bronze, gunmetal and machined faces each change the character of a vehicle substantially. A set of bronze wheels on a dark car reads completely differently than the factory silver did.

Calipers are the finishing touch. Painted calipers in red, black, yellow or a color matched to the vehicle show through open wheel designs and pull the whole corner together. Done properly they are prepped, painted and heat-cured, not brush-painted in place.

Worth knowing

Powder coat or paint? It depends on the wheel.

Powder coating is more durable and the better choice for most refinishing, especially on a daily driver facing salt and brake heat. But it involves heat curing, which is not appropriate for every wheel — some multi-piece and specialty wheels need a different approach. We look at what you have and recommend the process that suits the wheel rather than the one that is easiest.

Questions

Frequently asked.

Can curb rash be repaired or do I need new wheels?

Cosmetic curb rash on the lip and face is repairable in most cases. The wheel is repaired, reprofiled and refinished. Cracked or bent wheels are a different matter and are a structural safety issue, not a cosmetic one.

Is powder coating better than painting wheels?

For most wheels, yes. Powder coat is cured onto the wheel and is considerably more resistant to chips, brake heat and road salt than wet paint. Some multi-piece and specialty wheels are not suited to the heat curing process, which we check first.

How long does wheel refinishing take?

The wheels have to come off the vehicle and go through strip, repair, finish and cure stages, so plan on the car being down for a few days. We give you a specific timeline when you book.

What color should I do my calipers?

Red is the most common and reads sporty. Black is the most subtle and works on almost anything. Yellow and other bright colors are bold and depend heavily on the wheel design and how much of the caliper shows through it. We can hold samples up against your wheels.

Will painted calipers survive brake heat?

When they are properly cleaned, prepped and heat-cured with the right coating, yes. Brush-painted calipers done without prep are the ones that flake and discolor within a season.

Is coating my wheels worth it?

If you have painted or powder-coated wheels and you are tired of scrubbing brake dust, yes. Coated wheels release dust far more easily and resist the baked-on buildup that permanently stains a finish.
